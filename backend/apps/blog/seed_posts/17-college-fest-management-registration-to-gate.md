College fest management means running four things well at once: a committee with clear roles, a budget backed by sponsors, online registration for competitions and entry, and a gate where volunteers check passes quickly. Plan backwards from the fest date, give every task one owner, and use one system for registration, QR passes and check-in so the numbers you report to the college and sponsors are real.

This guide follows a fest from the first committee meeting to the sponsor report. If you mainly need help designing the registration form, read our guide to [online registration forms for college fests](/blog/online-registration-forms-college-fests-koshi-province) first.

## How should you set up the fest committee?

Give each area one owner who reports to the coordinator, and write the roles down. Fests go wrong when "everyone" is responsible for the gate.

| Role | Responsible for | Works with |
|---|---|---|
| Coordinator | Overall plan, decisions, college administration | Faculty adviser |
| Finance | Budget, sponsor money, payments, receipts | Coordinator, sponsors |
| Sponsorship | Sponsor packages, deliverables, reports | Finance, publicity |
| Registration and tickets | Event page, forms, ticket types, promo codes | Publicity, gate |
| Competitions | Rules, judges, schedules, results | Registration |
| Publicity | Posters, social media, college outreach | Registration |
| Gate and security | Volunteers, scanners, entry rules, crowd flow | Registration |
| Stage and logistics | Venue, sound, stage, power, food stalls | Coordinator |

Agree early who can change ticket prices or rules. One person should have the final say on the event page.

## How do you plan the budget and sponsors?

Start with costs, then decide how much must come from sponsors, entry fees and the college. A simple budget sheet with expected and actual columns is enough, as long as finance updates it every week.

Typical lines:

- **Costs:** venue or ground setup, sound and lights, stage, decoration, prizes, judges' honorarium, performers, printing, food, security, volunteers' T-shirts.
- **Income:** college contribution, sponsors, entry tickets, competition fees, stall rentals.

Sponsors in Koshi Province are often local businesses: banks, telecom dealers, cafés, institutes and shops. Offer clear packages and promise only what you can deliver. Real footfall numbers from check-ins are more convincing than estimates, and they make the next year's sponsorship easier. ## How do you handle registration for competitions and entry?

Separate competition entries from general entry. They need different information and often different deadlines.

- **Competitions:** collect team name, members, college, contact number and category in the registration form. Close entries a few days before the fest so the competitions team can make schedules.
- **General entry:** keep the form short — name, phone, email and college — so students finish it in under a minute.
- **Group entries:** let one person register several people in one order, with a name on each pass.

Share one event link across all colleges, and give each partner college its own promo code so you can see where registrations come from.

## Which ticket types and gate rules work for college fests?

Use ticket types to separate groups, and gate notes to tell volunteers what to check. This setup works for most fests:

| Ticket type | For | Price idea | Gate note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Host college student | Your own students | Free or low | "Check college ID" |
| Other college student | Students from other campuses | Student price | "Check student ID" |
| General | Outsiders and family | Full price | — |
| Participant | Registered competitors | Free | "Send to the competitions desk" |
| Guest and judges | Invited guests, judges, sponsors | Free, hidden | "Guest — welcome desk" |

## How do volunteers run the gate?

Treat gate volunteers as a team that needs training, not as extra hands on the day. A well-briefed volunteer with a phone handles a crowd better than an untrained one with a printed list.

1. **Create a login for each volunteer** and assign them to a gate.
2. **Brief them on scan results:** valid, already scanned (duplicate), wrong gate, unpaid or amount to collect.
3. **Run a test scan** the day before with printed passes.
4. **Put a committee member at each gate** to handle disputes and walk-ins.
5. **Bring power banks** and a spare phone for each busy gate.
6. **Keep a walk-in lane** where people register on their phone and join the scanning queue.

## What happens on fest day?

Write a simple run sheet and share it with every team lead. A rough example:

| Time | Task | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | Sound check, stage, stalls, sponsor branding | Stage and logistics |
| 1 hour before gates | Volunteers at gates, test scans, power banks charged | Gate and security |
| Gates open | Scan entry; walk-in lane open; payment counter if needed | Gate and security |
| During the fest | Watch the live count inside; announcements to staff | Coordinator |
| Competitions | Check participants in at the competitions desk | Competitions |
| Voting rounds | Open and close audience voting on time | Competitions |
| Closing | Prize distribution, exit flow, clean-up | All |

Watch the number of people inside against the venue's capacity, especially in the evening when the main performance draws a crowd.

## How do you run voting for competitions?

Run audience voting so it rewards performers, not the biggest group of friends with spare phones. The safest option at a fest is to tie each vote to a fest pass and allow only checked-in attendees to vote.

- Announce who can vote, how many votes each person has and when voting closes.
- If judges also score, publish the weighting before the competition starts.
- Keep results hidden until voting closes, then reveal them on the big screen.

## How does the crew stay coordinated?

Use one channel for the crew, separate from the student WhatsApp groups where announcements get lost. ## What should you do after the fest?

Close the loop within a week while everyone still remembers the details.

- **Export the attendee list** with check-in times and promo codes as a CSV.
- **Prepare the sponsor report:** registrations, check-ins, which colleges attended, and photos of their branding.
- **Settle accounts:** match receipts to the budget and hand over to the college.
- **Write down lessons:** what slowed the gate, which promotion worked, what to change next year.
- **Thank** volunteers, sponsors, judges and partner colleges.

## Key takeaways

- Give every area of college fest management one owner, and write the roles down.
- Separate competition entries from general entry, and use ticket types and gate notes for each group.
- Train volunteers as gate staff with their own logins, a briefing and a test scan.
- Tie competition votes to fest passes and publish the rules before voting opens.
- Use real check-in numbers in your sponsor report.
