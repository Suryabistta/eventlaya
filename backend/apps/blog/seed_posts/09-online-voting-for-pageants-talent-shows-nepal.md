Online voting for pageants and talent shows lets the audience vote from their phones, usually by opening a link or scanning a QR code shown on stage, and the results are counted automatically. For the vote to be trusted, each person should get one vote (tied to a ticket or an account), the rules and voting window should be announced in advance, and the audience vote should be clearly separated from — or openly combined with — the judges' scores.

## How does live audience voting work?

The organizer creates a poll with the contestants or performances as options, opens it at the right moment, and attendees vote on their phones. Results are tallied instantly and can be shown on a projector or LED screen.

A typical sequence at a talent show:

1. Before the event, create a poll such as "Crowd favourite performance" with each act as an option.
2. Decide who can vote: everyone with the link, or only people with a valid ticket.
3. During the show, display the poll link or QR code on screen.
4. Open voting after the last performance, or keep it open throughout.
5. Close voting at an announced time.
6. Reveal results on the big screen and announce the winner.

The same approach works for a college band competition, a best project award at an exhibition, a dance battle, or a crowd-favourite award at a pageant.

## What makes a vote fair?

A fair vote has clear eligibility, one vote per person, a fixed voting window and a transparent result. If participants and audience can see those four things, disputes are much less likely.

### One person, one vote

The biggest risk in online voting is the same person voting again and again from different browsers or devices. Tie each vote to something a person has only one of:

- **A ticket pass** — one vote per pass, so only people who registered for the event can vote.
- **A signed-in account** — one vote per account.

Fully open polls (anyone with the link) are useful for low-stakes fun, but avoid them for anything with a prize.

### Clear rules, announced early

Publish the rules on the event page and announce them on stage before voting opens:

- Who can vote.
- How many votes each person gets.
- When voting opens and closes.
- How ties are resolved.
- How audience votes and judges' scores are combined, if both are used.

### A fixed voting window

Open and close voting at announced times. Changing the deadline while one contestant is ahead looks unfair, even if it is not.

### Transparent results

Show the final count, not only the winner's name. If you do not show live results during voting, reveal the full results right after it closes.

## Should you use judges, audience votes or both?

Use judges for skill-based decisions and audience votes for popularity awards. When you combine them, announce the weighting before the event starts.

| Method | Strengths | Weaknesses | Works well for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Judges' panel | Expert, consistent criteria | Small group; can face bias claims | Main titles, technical categories |
| Ticket-holder audience vote | One vote per pass; only people present | Rewards contestants with more friends in the hall | Crowd favourite, People's Choice |
| Open online poll | Easy to join; large reach | Easier to manipulate | Fun polls, low-stakes questions |
| Combined (judges + audience) | Balances skill and popularity | Needs a clear, pre-announced formula | Talent shows, college competitions |

Some competitions also use paid or SMS-based voting. These raise additional fairness questions, since supporters who spend more can buy more votes, so if you consider them, be open about how votes are counted and where any money goes.

## How do you keep judges' scoring fair alongside the audience vote?

Give judges written criteria and a score sheet before the show, and keep their scores separate from the audience poll until both are final.

- Agree criteria in advance, for example talent, presentation, confidence and audience engagement.
- Ask judges to declare any connection to a contestant, such as a relative, student or teammate.
- Have someone other than the judges total the scores.
- Keep the sheets after the event in case of questions.

## How should you show results on the big screen?

Use a presenter view designed for a projector: large text, clear bars, and no admin controls visible. Decide in advance whether to show results live or only after voting closes.

- **Live results** build excitement and keep the audience engaged.
- **Hidden until close** avoids a bandwagon effect where people vote for whoever is already leading.

Test the presenter screen on the actual projector or LED wall during the sound check. Small text that looks fine on a laptop can be unreadable from the back of a hall.

## What practical problems should you plan for?

Most voting problems on the night are about the network, the screen or unclear instructions, not the software.

- **Mobile data congestion:** a full hall can slow mobile data. Give voters a few minutes, and consider a dedicated Wi-Fi network if the venue has one.
- **Unclear instructions:** show the QR code and a short link together, and have the host explain the steps once, slowly.
- **Voting before all acts perform:** if voting is open throughout, early acts may get more or fewer votes. Consider opening voting after the final act.
- **Ticket-holder polls:** remind people that they need their pass or account, and make sure registration is complete before voting opens. Our guide to [online registration forms for college fests](/blog/online-registration-forms-college-fests-koshi-province) covers registration setup.
- **Power cuts:** keep the projector and router on backup power.
