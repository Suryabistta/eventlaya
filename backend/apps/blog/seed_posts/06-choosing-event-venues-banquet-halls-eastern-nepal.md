To choose an event venue in Eastern Nepal, start with your event type and realistic headcount, then shortlist venues that match on capacity for your seating style, location and parking, power backup, and availability in your season. Visit in person, ask exactly what the price includes, and get every promise in writing. In cities like Biratnagar, Itahari and Dharan you can choose from banquets and party palaces, hotel ballrooms, conference halls and auditoriums, resorts and lawns, open grounds and community centers, and each suits a different kind of event.

## Which type of venue suits your event?

Match the venue type to the format of your event first, and the budget second. A beautiful banquet can still be the wrong choice for a concert, and an open ground can be the wrong choice in July.

| Venue type | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Banquets and party palaces | Weddings, receptions, award nights, seminars, launches | Busy in wedding seasons; in-house catering may be compulsory |
| Hotel ballrooms | Conferences, gala dinners, corporate events with overnight guests | Usually higher cost; strict timings |
| Auditoriums and conference halls | Talks, seminars, performances, prize distributions | Fixed seating; limited space for stalls or food |
| Resorts and lawns | Day outings, receptions, outdoor parties | Weather; distance from town; transport for guests |
| Open grounds | Concerts, fairs, expos, sports events | Monsoon, heat, permissions, toilets, power, security |
| Community centers | Local programmes, meetings, cultural events | Basic facilities; you may need to bring sound and decoration |

If you are not sure, write down three things: how many people, seated or standing, and indoors or outdoors. That usually narrows the list quickly.

## How do you work out the right capacity?

Use the capacity for your exact seating style, not the venue's headline number. A hall that holds a large standing crowd may seat far fewer people at round tables.

- **Theatre style** (rows of chairs) fits the most people.
- **Round tables** for dinners need much more space per person.
- **Standing crowds** for concerts need clear space at the front and safe exits.
- **Stalls and exhibition booths** take floor space away from the audience.

Leave room for the stage, sound desk, buffet, registration desk and walkways. Then set your ticket quantity safely below the limit so the room never overfills. When you sell tickets online, you can cap each ticket type's quantity to match.

## What should you check during a site visit?

Visit at the same time of day as your event, and walk the route your attendees will take from the road to their seat. A short checklist:

1. **Access and parking:** Is the venue easy to find? Where do bikes, cars and buses park?
2. **Power backup:** Is there a generator or inverter, what does it cover (lights, sound, fans, AC), and is fuel included?
3. **Sound and acoustics:** Does the hall echo? Is there a house sound system, or will you bring one?
4. **Ventilation and cooling:** Fans or AC matter in the Terai heat, especially in April and May.
5. **Toilets:** Are there enough, and are there separate facilities for men and women?
6. **Entrances and exits:** How many doors are there? Can you set up a separate VIP or staff entry?
7. **Mobile signal:** Check data on more than one network near the entrance, because your gate scanner may rely on it.
8. **Rain plan:** For lawns and grounds, where does the event move if it rains?
9. **Neighbours and noise:** Are there time limits on amplified sound?

Take photos and notes so you can compare venues side by side later.

## What questions should you ask before signing?

Ask what is included, what is extra, and what happens if plans change. Misunderstandings about these three things cause most venue disputes.

- What exactly does the quoted price include: hall, chairs, tables, stage, lights, sound, decoration, catering, cleaning?
- Is outside catering or decoration allowed, or must you use the venue's own?
- What are the setup and vacate times, and is there a charge for running late?
- How much advance is needed, and what is the cancellation or date-change policy?
- Who is your contact on the day, and what is their phone number?
- Are taxes and service charges included in the price?
- Does the venue have any rules on music, alcohol, fireworks or end time?

Get the answers in a written agreement or receipt, with the date, hall name and timings clearly stated.

## When should you book a venue in Eastern Nepal?

Book as early as you can for wedding-season and festival-season dates, and keep the monsoon in mind for anything outdoors.

| Period | Venue situation | Advice |
|---|---|---|
| April–May | Hot in the Terai | Prefer indoor, ventilated halls |
| June–September | Monsoon | Avoid open grounds and lawns, or have an indoor backup |
| Late Sept–Nov | Dashain and Tihar | Venues and vendors busy; many people travel home |
| Mangsir (Nov–Dec) | Main wedding season | Banquets book early; reserve well ahead |
| Magh–Falgun (Jan–Mar) | Second wedding season, mild weather | Popular for outdoor events; book early |

In the hills, such as Dhankuta or Ilam, also plan for cool evenings in winter. Our [Koshi Province guide](/blog/event-management-in-koshi-province-jhapa-morang-sunsari) covers smaller towns and hill venues.

## How do you compare venues fairly?

Compare total cost for your event, not the hall rent alone. One venue may look cheaper but charge separately for sound, generator fuel, decoration or extra hours.

Make a simple comparison sheet with these columns: capacity for your layout, total quoted cost, what is included, extras, parking, power backup, distance for most guests and availability on your date. Fill it in for each venue after the site visit.

## How does the venue affect your gate and check-in plan?

The venue's layout decides how many gates you need and where queues will form. A hall with one narrow door needs a different plan from a ground with three entrances.

- Use the venue's main door for general entry and a side door for VIPs or performers if possible.
- Put the registration desk outside the hall, not in the doorway.
- If mobile signal is weak at the entrance, plan for offline scanning and ask attendees to save their pass.
- Count people going in and out if the hall has a strict capacity.

## Summary

- Choose the venue type by event format first, then by budget.
- Use capacity for your exact seating style and stay safely below it.
- Visit in person and check power backup, parking, toilets, exits and mobile signal.
- Get inclusions, extras, timings and cancellation terms in writing.
- Book early for Mangsir and Magh–Falgun, and avoid outdoor venues in the monsoon.
