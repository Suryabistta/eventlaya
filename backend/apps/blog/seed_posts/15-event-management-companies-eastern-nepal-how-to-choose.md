To choose an event management company in Eastern Nepal, shortlist two or three firms through venues, vendors and past clients, ask each for an itemized quote, check their recent work and registration details, and sign a written agreement with a clear payment schedule. If what you really need is registration, tickets and a smooth gate rather than stage production, event software and a few reliable vendors may be enough on their own.

This guide covers how to pick a company in Itahari, Dharan, Biratnagar, Damak, Birtamod and nearby towns. For the broader question of whether to hire a company at all, read [event management company vs event software](/blog/event-management-company-vs-event-software).

## What does an event management company usually handle?

Most event management companies in Eastern Nepal coordinate the physical side of an event: venue, stage, sound and lights, decoration, catering, photography, anchors or performers, and the team on the day. Some also handle sponsorship, promotion and permits.

Services vary a lot between firms. Some are decorators who also manage events, some focus on concerts and stage production, and some handle corporate programmes and conferences. Before comparing prices, make sure each company is quoting for the same scope.

## Where do you find event management companies in Koshi Province?

The best leads usually come from people who have worked with a company recently. Advertising tells you what a company wants to sell; a venue manager tells you who turns up on time.

Good places to look:

- **Venues:** banquet and party palace managers in Itahari, Biratnagar, Dharan, Damak and Birtamod see many companies at work. Ask who they would recommend and why.
- **Vendors:** sound, lighting, decoration and catering providers know which companies pay on time and plan properly.
- **Past clients:** colleges, schools, businesses, clubs and NGOs that held similar events in your town.
- **Social media:** Facebook and Instagram pages with photos and videos of recent events, ideally with tagged clients or venues you can check.
- **Business networks:** your local chamber of commerce or business association may know firms that have worked with members.

If you are also choosing a venue, browse banquets, party palaces and vendors on the Eventlaya [marketplace](/marketplace) and send inquiries directly to the listing owners.

## How do you shortlist companies?

Compare companies on evidence, not on the size of their social media following. This table shows what to look for.

| What to check | What a good sign looks like |
|---|---|
| Similar events | They have run events of your type and size recently, and can show them |
| Local knowledge | They know venues, vendors and permission steps in your town |
| Team | A named lead who will be on site on the day |
| Written quote | Itemized, with quantities, exclusions and validity |
| Vendor relationships | They tell you which vendors they will use and let you meet them |
| Paperwork | Registration and PAN/VAT details, bills for payments |
| Communication | They reply clearly and in writing, and ask about your goals and budget |

## What should you ask in the first meeting?

Use the first meeting to see how they think, not only what they charge. Bring your date, expected guests, budget range and must-haves.

1. What similar events have you run in the last year, and can we speak to the client?
2. Who will lead our event on the day, and how many of your staff will be on site?
3. Which vendors will you use for sound, stage, decoration and catering?
4. What is included in your management fee, and what is charged separately?
5. How do you handle a change of date because of rain, a strike or a festival clash?
6. Who arranges permissions from the ward, municipality or police, if they are needed?
7. How will registration, tickets and entry work, and who keeps the attendee list?
8. What do you need from us, and by when?

## How should you read an event company's quote?

A useful quote lists every service on its own line, with quantities and units, so you can compare companies line by line. A single lump sum makes comparison impossible and changes hard to price.

| Line item | What to check |
|---|---|
| Venue | Hall or ground, hours, power backup, parking, what is included |
| Stage, sound and lights | Size, equipment list, technician on site, setup and teardown times |
| Decoration | Theme, flowers or fabric, entrance, stage backdrop |
| Catering | Menu, per-plate basis, minimum guaranteed count, service staff |
| Photography and video | Hours, number of people, delivery time and format |
| Anchors and performers | Names or level, duration, travel and accommodation |
| Security and volunteers | Number of people, hours, role at the gate |
| Printing and branding | Banners, backdrops, ID cards, quantities |
| Permits and fees | Which permissions and who pays the fees |
| Registration and ticketing | Tool used, who manages it, who owns the data |
| Management fee | Fixed amount or percentage, and what it covers |
| Taxes | Whether VAT is included, and that a bill will be issued |

Ask how long the quote is valid, especially in Mangsir and Magh–Falgun, when wedding-season demand can push up venue and vendor prices.

## What should the agreement and payment schedule include?

Put the agreement in writing even if you know the company personally. A one- or two-page document prevents most disputes.

Include:

- **Scope:** the itemized services and quantities from the final quote.
- **Dates and times:** setup, event and teardown.
- **Payment schedule:** for example an advance on signing, a payment when vendors are confirmed and the balance after the event. Link each payment to a milestone and get a bill or receipt every time.
- **Changes:** how added or removed services are priced.
- **Postponement and cancellation:** what happens to advances if the date moves because of weather, a strike, an official holiday or a decision by the authorities.
- **Responsibilities:** who handles permits, security, damage to the venue and vendor payments.
- **Data:** that attendee lists, ticket sales and photos belong to you.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For large contracts, ask someone with legal or business experience to read the agreement.

## How do you check that a company is genuine?

Verify before you pay an advance. A genuine company will not mind.

- Ask for its company or firm registration and PAN or VAT details, and make sure bills carry them.
- Visit its office or store if it has one.
- Call two recent clients and ask what went well and what did not.
- Ask to see photos or videos of an event whose venue or client you can confirm.
- Check reviews and comments on its pages, including the negative ones and how it replied.

## What are the red flags?

Walk away, or slow down, if you see these:

- A large advance demanded immediately, in cash, without a bill.
- No itemized quote, or a quote that changes after you agree.
- No named person responsible for your event on the day.
- Vague answers about which vendors will be used.
- Promises of guaranteed crowds, sponsors or media coverage.
- Reluctance to put postponement or cancellation terms in writing.
- They want to keep the attendee list or ticket money without giving you access.

## When is event software enough without a company?

Software alone is often enough when the hard part of your event is people, not production. If your venue provides sound, seating and catering, the remaining work is usually registration, tickets, payment and the gate.

That often applies to:

- seminars, workshops and training sessions;
- college fests run by a student committee with a faculty adviser;
- club events, meetups and community programmes;
- repeat events where you already know reliable vendors.

## How do a company and software work together?

For many large events the best answer is both: the company runs production, and the organizer keeps registration, ticketing and the gate in their own account.

That way:

- the attendee list and ticket sales stay with you, the organizer;
- the company's staff can be added as gate staff and assigned to gates;
- everyone sees the same live check-in numbers on the day;
- you can reuse the same setup for next year, with or without the same company.

If you choose this route, agree in writing who manages the event page and who has access to the attendee data. You can [create a free account](/signup) and add the company's team as staff when you are ready.

## Key takeaways

- Find event management companies through venues, vendors and past clients, not just advertising.
- Compare itemized quotes line by line, for the same scope, with quantities and exclusions.
- Agree a written payment schedule tied to milestones, with bills for every payment.
- Verify registration details, recent events and references before paying an advance.
- If your main need is registration, tickets and the gate, software and good vendors may be enough.
