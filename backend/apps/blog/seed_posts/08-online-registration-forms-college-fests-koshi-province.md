An online registration form for a college fest is a single web link where students sign up, choose a ticket or competition, and receive a confirmation they can show at the gate. For college fests in Koshi Province — in Biratnagar, Itahari, Dharan, Damak, Birtamod and beyond — a good form asks only what you need, separates ticket types (your students, outside students, guests), and is easy to open on a phone from a Facebook post or a class Viber group.

## Why use an online form instead of paper sign-up sheets?

Online forms collect clean data in one place, let you cap numbers automatically, and give every participant a confirmation. Paper sheets at a department desk get lost, are hard to read, and cannot tell you how many seats are left.

With an online form you can:

- Share one link across colleges, clubs and social media.
- Close registration automatically when a competition is full.
- Contact everyone with one email or message list.
- Check people in at the gate instead of reading names off paper.

## What should a college fest registration form ask?

Ask for the minimum needed to contact the participant, place them in the right event, and verify them at the gate. Every extra question reduces how many people finish the form.

Here is a practical starting set, with the field type that suits each question:

| Question | Field type | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Full name | Short text | Name on the pass and certificates |
| Phone number | Phone | Event-day calls and WhatsApp or Viber updates |
| Email | Email | Pass delivery and confirmations |
| College or school | Dropdown (with "Other") | Separating internal and outside participants |
| Faculty / year | Dropdown | Useful for department-wise prizes |
| Competitions joining | Checkbox | Lets one person enter several events |
| Team name (for team events) | Short text | Grouping team members |
| T-shirt size (if included) | Radio | Ordering merchandise |
| Dietary preference (if food is served) | Radio | Catering numbers |
| Anything we should know? | Paragraph | Accessibility needs, questions |

A few tips:

- **Use dropdowns and checkboxes** instead of free text wherever possible. "Itahari", "itahari" and "Ithari" become three different answers in a spreadsheet.
- **Ask for date of birth only if you need it**, for example for an age-limited competition. Use a date field so the format is consistent.
- **Mark only essential questions as required.**
- **Write questions in plain English**, and add Nepali where it helps your audience.

## How should you set up ticket types for a college fest?

Create one ticket type for each group that pays a different price, enters through a different gate, or needs a different check. Most fests need three to five.

Typical ticket types for a college fest:

- **Internal students** — free or low price; staff check college ID at the gate.
- **Outside students** — separate price and limit, so your own students are not crowded out.
- **Early Bird** — a lower price for a limited time to encourage early sign-ups.
- **Guest / alumni** — for family members and former students.
- **VIP / sponsor** — a hidden ticket type shared only with sponsors and chief guests.

For each ticket type, decide the price, how many are available, and when sales open and close. On Eventlaya you can also hide ticket types from the public page, set a maximum number of tickets per order, and choose whether to show or hide "spots left".

## How can promo codes help a fest?

Promo codes let you give a discount or unlock a hidden ticket for a specific group without changing your public prices. They are useful when different clubs, departments or partner colleges bring participants.

Ideas that work well:

- A code for a partner college that gives a percentage off.
- A code for volunteers that unlocks a free hidden ticket.
- A code for a sponsor with a fixed usage limit for their guests.
- An expiring code for the first week of registration.

Whatever tool you use, give each code a usage limit and an expiry date so it cannot spread beyond the group it was meant for.

## How do team and group registrations work?

For team competitions — debates, hackathons, quizzes, band contests — let one person register the team and enter each member's name. Each member should still get their own pass, so they can enter separately.

## How do you get students to actually register?

Share the link where students already are, and make the first screen clear: what, when, where, and how much. Most college fests in Eastern Nepal spread through Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp and Viber groups.

- Post the registration link in class and club groups, not only on the college page.
- Put the date in both Bikram Sambat and AD, for example on the poster and the event page.
- Use a short countdown to the end of early bird pricing.
- Ask each department representative to share the link with their class.
- Print a QR code that opens the registration page on posters around campus.

## When should registration open and close?

Open registration early enough for students to plan, and close it when you need final numbers for food, certificates and seating. Leave room for walk-ins if your venue allows.

Timing tips for Koshi Province:

- **Avoid the Dashain–Tihar period** for closing dates; many students travel home and will miss reminders.
- **Check the exam calendar** of the colleges you are inviting, not just your own.
- **Watch the weather.** Outdoor fests during the monsoon (roughly June to September) risk rain and road problems, especially for participants travelling from the hills.
- **Plan walk-ins.** If your tool keeps registration open during the event, a walk-in desk can register people on the spot.
