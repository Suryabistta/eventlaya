Event management software in Nepal is a web or mobile tool that handles the paperwork of an event in one place: an event page, online registration, tickets, QR-code passes, gate check-in and attendee reports. For organizers in Nepal, the features that matter most are simple registration that works on any phone, QR passes that arrive by email, a gate scanner that copes with weak mobile data, and a payment option that fits how people actually pay, whether that is cash at the gate or a mobile wallet.

## What does event management software actually do?

It replaces the mix of paper lists, Facebook comments, phone calls and Excel sheets that most events in Nepal still run on. A good tool covers the full life of an event, from the first announcement to the final report.

Typical jobs it handles:

- **Event page:** one shareable link with the description, schedule, speakers, venue map and FAQs.
- **Registration:** a form that collects names, phone numbers and any custom questions you need, such as college name or T-shirt size.
- **Tickets:** different ticket types (General, Student, VIP, Early Bird, free entry) with their own price and quantity.
- **Passes:** a unique QR code per attendee, sent by email and viewable on screen.
- **Check-in:** staff scan passes at the gate with a phone, and duplicates are flagged.
- **Reports:** registrations, check-ins and an attendee list you can export as CSV.

Bigger platforms add team tools, live audience voting, contest and pageant voting with judges, budgets and a vendor directory.

## Which features matter most for events in Nepal?

The features that matter most are the ones that survive local conditions: crowded mobile networks, mixed payment habits and volunteers who have never used a scanner before. Use this table as a checklist when comparing tools.

| Feature | Why it matters in Nepal |
|---|---|
| Works in a phone browser | Most attendees register from a phone after seeing a link on Facebook, TikTok, WhatsApp or Viber. |
| QR pass by email and on screen | Attendees can show the pass even if they lose the email or have no data at the gate. |
| Offline scanning | Mobile data often slows down when hundreds of people gather in one place. |
| Multiple gates and staff logins | Concerts, fests and expos often need a VIP gate, a student gate and an exit. |
| Duplicate-pass alerts | Screenshots of the same pass get shared; the scanner should catch the second use. |
| Flexible ticket types and promo codes | Student discounts, early-bird pricing and free guest passes are common. |
| Nepali-friendly payments | Many people still pay cash, and many prefer eSewa or Khalti. |
| CSV export | Sponsors, colleges and committees usually ask for the list afterwards. |

If you only check three things, check offline scanning, duplicate alerts and how payments work. Those are where events in Nepal usually run into trouble. Our guide on [QR code tickets and gate check-in](/blog/qr-code-tickets-gate-check-in-nepal) goes into the gate side in detail.

## How do attendees pay for event tickets in Nepal?

Most events in Nepal today collect money in one of three ways: cash at the gate, a bank or wallet transfer that the organizer confirms by hand, or online checkout through a wallet such as eSewa or Khalti. Each has trade-offs.

- **Pay at the venue:** simplest to set up and familiar to everyone, but you need a counter, change and someone trustworthy to handle cash.
- **Manual transfer:** attendees send money and a screenshot, and you match them up. It works for small numbers but becomes a full-time job beyond that.
- **Integrated wallet checkout:** payment and ticket happen together, which saves the most time, but check exactly which wallets and fees a tool supports before you rely on it.

## Do you need software for a small event?

Not always. For a 30-person workshop where everyone is known, a form and a printed list can be enough. Software starts paying for itself when any of these are true:

1. You expect more people than you personally know.
2. You charge for entry or have more than one ticket type.
3. More than one person will check people in.
4. You need numbers afterwards for a sponsor, college or committee.
5. You worry about fake or shared tickets.

A free plan is a low-risk way to try this on a small event before a big one.

## How do you choose the right event management software?

Choose the tool that fits your biggest event of the year, then check it is simple enough for your smallest one. A practical way to decide:

1. **List your must-haves.** For example: student tickets, two gates, offline scanning, CSV export.
2. **Register as an attendee.** Go through the form on a cheap Android phone with mobile data. If it is confusing for you, it will be confusing for your audience.
3. **Test the scanner.** Print a few passes, scan them, scan one twice and see what happens.
4. **Check payments honestly.** Confirm what is live today, not what is planned.
5. **Ask about support.** If something breaks on event day, who answers, and in what language?
6. **Look at the exit.** Make sure you can export your attendee data whenever you want.

## How do you set up your first event online?

Setting up an event takes an afternoon if your details are ready. A typical sequence:

1. Write the event description, date, time and venue. Add the Nepali (BS) date next to the AD date.
2. Build the registration form with only the questions you truly need.
3. Create ticket types, quantities and sales windows. Add promo codes for students or sponsors.
4. Publish and share the single event link on social media and messaging groups.
5. Add gate staff, assign them to gates, and do a test scan a day before.
6. On the day, watch check-ins live and keep an eye on the inside-the-venue count.
7. Afterwards, export the attendee list and review the numbers.
