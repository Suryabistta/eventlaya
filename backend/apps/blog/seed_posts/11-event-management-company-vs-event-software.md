An event management company plans and runs an event for you — venue, vendors, stage, sound, decoration and on-site coordination — while event software gives your own team tools for registration, tickets, check-in and coordination. For organizers in Eastern Nepal, software is often enough when you have a willing team and a straightforward event, a company is the better choice when the event is large, high-stakes or heavy on production, and many events work best with both.

## What does an event management company do?

An event management company takes responsibility for planning and delivering the event, usually for a fee on top of vendor costs. You describe what you want, and they coordinate the people and equipment needed to make it happen.

Typical services include:

- Venue search and booking.
- Stage, sound, lighting and LED screens.
- Decoration, branding and backdrops.
- Catering coordination.
- Artist and anchor booking.
- Permissions and coordination with local authorities.
- On-site management on the day, including setup and teardown.

Event companies differ: some focus on weddings and private functions, others on concerts, corporate programmes or college events. Their strengths vary, so it helps to ask what kind of events they handle most often.

## What does event software do?

Event software gives organizers tools to manage the attendee side of an event: the event page, registration, tickets, passes, gate check-in and reporting. It does not provide people or equipment on site.

Common features include:

- An event page you can share as one link.
- Registration forms and ticket types.
- QR code passes sent by email.
- Gate check-in with a phone scanner.
- Attendee lists, exports and analytics.
- Team tools such as staff logins, task boards and chat.

## Event management company vs event software: how do they compare?

A company sells time, experience and a vendor network; software sells tools your team uses. The table below shows where each fits.

| Task | Event management company | Event software (your team) |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing and booking the venue | Handles it, often through existing contacts | Your team searches and books |
| Stage, sound, lights, decoration | Arranged and supervised | Your team hires and manages vendors |
| Event page and registration | Varies; may use their own tools | Built in and self-service |
| Tickets and QR passes | Varies | Built in |
| Gate check-in | Their staff, their method | Your volunteers with phone scanners |
| Attendee data and reports | Shared on request | Available to you directly |
| On-site problem solving | Experienced crew | Depends on your team |
| Cost structure | Management fee plus vendor costs | Subscription or free plan plus vendor costs |
| Control over details | Shared with the company | Fully with you |

## When is hiring an event management company the better choice?

Hire a company when the production is complex, the stakes are high, or your team lacks the time or experience to coordinate many vendors.

Situations where a company often makes sense:

- **Large concerts or public shows** with big stages, heavy sound, LED walls and crowd control.
- **Weddings and family functions** where the family wants to enjoy the day rather than manage vendors.
- **Corporate events and product launches** where the brand image must be polished and nobody internal can manage it full-time.
- **First-time large events** where you do not yet know which vendors are reliable.
- **Sponsor-heavy events** where branding commitments must be delivered exactly.
- **Tight timelines** when there is no time to build a volunteer team.

## When is event software enough on its own?

Software is often enough when your event is mostly about getting the right people registered, checked in and informed, and your own team can handle the venue and a few vendors.

Situations where software alone usually works:

- **College fests** run by a student committee with volunteers.
- **Club events, meetups and workshops** in a hall or hotel conference room.
- **Recurring events** such as monthly programmes, where your team already knows the routine.
- **Seminars and conferences** where the venue provides sound and seating.
- **Community and school programmes** with a clear organizing committee.

In these cases, the main work is registration, payment collection, gate check-in and keeping volunteers coordinated — exactly what software is built for. ## When should you combine a company and software?

Combine them when you want professional production on stage but direct control over your attendees, tickets and data. This split is common and works well.

Ways to combine:

- **The company runs production; you run attendees.** The company handles stage, sound, lights and decoration, while your team runs registration, passes and gate check-in on software you control.
- **The company uses software for you.** Some event companies use registration and check-in tools for their clients. Ask which tools they use and whether you will get access to the attendee data.
- **You coordinate; vendors deliver.** Your team uses software for tasks, budget and chat, and hires specialist vendors for each part of the production.

Keeping registration and attendee data in your own account is useful if you run the event every year — next year you start with last year's lists and numbers rather than from nothing.

## How do you decide for your event?

Answer a few honest questions about your event and your team, and the choice usually becomes clear.

1. **How big and complex is the production?** Large stages and heavy sound point toward a company.
2. **Does your team have time?** If nobody can coordinate vendors for weeks, hire help.
3. **Have you done this before?** Experience reduces the need for outside management.
4. **What is the budget?** Get itemized quotes from companies and compare them with direct vendor costs plus your team's time.
5. **Who needs to own the attendee data?** If you want it for future events, keep registration in your own hands.

## What should you ask before hiring a company?

Ask questions that turn a general promise into specific commitments.

- What exactly is included in the quote, item by item?
- Who will be the on-site lead on the day?
- Which vendors will be used, and can we meet them?
- How are changes, delays and cancellations handled?
- Which tools are used for registration and check-in, and do we get the data?
- Can you show examples of similar events you have managed?
