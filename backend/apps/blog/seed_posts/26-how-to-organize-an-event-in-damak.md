To organize an event in Damak, plan for a central-Jhapa audience: choose a date that avoids the main festival and wedding weeks and local holidays such as the Dhimal community's Sirijatra, book a venue with safe access and parking off the East-West Highway, and inform the ward office, the municipality and, for large public gatherings, the police and District Administration Office. Damak is one of the oldest and largest municipalities in Jhapa, and the highway runs straight through it, so most events here draw people from neighbouring towns as well as from the city itself.

## Why organize your event in Damak?

Damak is a municipality in Jhapa district, between the Ratuwa River to the east and the Maawa River to the west, with the Sivalik (Chure) hills to the north. The Mahendra (East-West) Highway bisects the town, which makes it easy to reach from both directions along the highway.

That position suits:

- **Central-Jhapa events** that want people from Damak, Urlabari, Lakhanpur and the surrounding rural municipalities in one place.
- **College and school events.** Damak has many schools and several campuses, including Damak Multiple Campus, a Tribhuvan University affiliated community campus founded in 2038 BS.
- **Community and cultural programmes.** Damak's population includes Brahmin, Chhetri, Dhimal, Limbu, Newar and Rai communities, among others, each with its own festivals and associations.
- **Agricultural and business events.** The town has a large livestock market at Pashuhat in ward 7, and many businesses serve the surrounding farming area.
- **Weddings, receptions and family functions**, which fill banquets and party palaces across Jhapa in the wedding seasons.

Damak is also known as home to many ex-Gurkha soldiers, so veterans' and welfare associations are another active group of organizers.

## Who will come to an event in Damak, and from where?

Expect a mix of town residents and people travelling in along the highway or from villages to the north and south. That affects your start time, your transport notes and how long you keep registration open.

Practical steps:

1. **Ask where people are coming from.** A "Which town are you coming from?" dropdown on the registration form shows your real catchment and helps with transport planning.
2. **Publish the start and end time clearly**, so people coming by bus or bike can plan their way home, especially in winter when it gets dark early.
3. **Plan for guests who fly in.** Bhadrapur Airport is about 52 km from Damak and Biratnagar Airport about 56 km by road (Nepal Traveller). Arrange pickups and keep a buffer in the programme in case a flight is delayed.
4. **Share an exact map pin.** Visitors who only know "near the highway" can lose time finding the gate.

## When is the best time to hold an event in Damak?

The easiest periods are outside the monsoon, the hottest pre-monsoon weeks and the big festival and wedding peaks. Use this as a rough guide and check the exact BS dates for your year.

| Period | What to expect in Damak | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Baisakh–Jestha (Apr–Jun) | Hot Terai afternoons | Prefer indoor or evening programmes; plan water and fans |
| Around Asar 2 (mid-June) | Dhimal Sirijatra; local public holiday in Damak | Avoid the day, or plan a programme that respects it |
| Asar–Bhadra (Jun–Sep) | Monsoon rain | Avoid open grounds; have an indoor backup |
| Asoj–Kartik (Sep–Nov) | Dashain, Tihar, Chhath; fairs in nearby villages | Audiences, vendors and staff are busy or travelling |
| Mangsir (Nov–Dec) | Main wedding season | Book banquets and vendors early |
| Poush–Magh (Dec–Feb) | Cool days, foggy mornings, Udhauli, Lhosar, Maghe Sankranti | Start later in the morning; check community festival dates |
| Magh–Falgun (Jan–Mar) | Second wedding season, pleasant weather | Good for events; book early |

Dashain fairs pull crowds to nearby villages too. One example is the long-running Santhal fair at Mangalbare in Kamal Rural Municipality, which has been held during Dashain since 1980 according to The Rising Nepal. A festival week is a hard time to compete for attention.

## What kind of venue works best in Damak?

Pick the venue by event type, then check access, parking, power and heat. The main options are:

- **Banquets and party palaces:** seminars, award nights, receptions and community programmes with a seated audience.
- **Hotel halls:** small conferences, trainings and meetings where some guests stay overnight.
- **School and college halls or grounds:** student events, competitions and community functions, with the institution's written approval.
- **Open grounds and covered halls:** sports tournaments, fairs and concerts, outside the monsoon and with a rain plan.

Before you pay a deposit, visit the venue at the same time of day as your event, test the generator or inverter, count toilets, and ask how many bikes and cars fit inside the compound. ## Do you need permission for an event in Damak?

For a large public event, inform your ward office and Damak Municipality first, then the local police. Jhapa's District Administration Office is at the district headquarters in Bhadrapur; for big public gatherings, ask whether it needs to be informed or give approval. Requirements depend on the size, place and type of event, so confirm them locally before you fix the date.

Things to ask about:

- Written approval for using a public ground or a school or college ground.
- Loudspeaker and music limits, and the latest finishing time.
- Crowd and traffic management if your gate is close to the highway.
- Permission for banners and hoardings in public places.

Keep copies of every letter at the gate on the day.

## How do you promote an event in Damak?

Give people one link to register and share it where they already talk. Posters still help near colleges and markets, but a link lets people confirm before they travel.

- Share the event link on Facebook, Instagram and TikTok, and in WhatsApp and Viber groups.
- Ask partner colleges, schools, clubs and community associations to share it with their members.
- Give each partner a promo code, so you can see which group brought the most registrations.
- Use an Early Bird ticket type with a clear end date to get commitments early.

## How do you plan around the highway in Damak?

Treat the highway as part of your event plan, not just the way in. Because the East-West Highway runs through the middle of Damak, venues near it are easy to find but need care at the gate.

- **Keep queues off the road.** Put the scanning point inside the compound and use rope lines so people do not spill onto the highway.
- **Plan parking for bikes.** Many attendees arrive on motorbikes; mark a parking area and put a volunteer there.
- **Stagger arrivals.** Ask attendees to arrive in a window (for example, 4:00–4:45 pm) instead of all at the start time.
- **Check road works.** The Damak Ring Road is still under construction; check access routes the week before the event and update the map pin if needed.
- **Plan the exit.** When an evening event ends, hundreds of people join the highway at once. Have volunteers at the gate and announce the end time in advance.

## How do you run check-in on event day?

Use QR passes, at least two scanning phones and a separate counter for walk-ins and payments. When a bus or a convoy of bikes arrives, a slow gate is the first thing people notice.

1. Brief gate staff the day before and do a test scan at the venue.
2. Use separate lines for pre-registered guests and walk-ins.
3. If people pay at the venue, put the payment counter before the gate, not inside it.
4. Watch for duplicate passes; screenshots get shared.
5. Keep an eye on the live count of people inside if the hall has a capacity limit.
