A QR code ticket is a digital pass with a unique code that gate staff scan with a phone to let an attendee in. Using QR code tickets in Nepal replaces paper tickets and printed guest lists: each attendee gets their own pass by email or on screen, staff scan it at the gate, and the system marks it as used so the same pass cannot be used twice. You do not need special hardware — phone cameras and a clear check-in plan are enough for most college fests, concerts, conferences and club events.

## How do QR code tickets work?

Each registration creates a pass with a unique code linked to one attendee and one ticket type. When the code is scanned, the scanner looks it up and tells staff whether the pass is valid, already used, or not allowed at that gate.

A typical flow looks like this:

1. The attendee registers on the event page and picks a ticket type (General, Student, VIP, free entry and so on).
2. A pass with a QR code is created and sent by email, and can also be opened on a pass page.
3. On event day, gate staff scan the code with a phone.
4. The scanner shows the result: valid, already checked in, wrong gate for this ticket type, or not found.
5. The check-in is recorded, so the organizer can see how many people have arrived.

The code is only an identifier; the protection comes from checking it against the attendee list, so a forwarded screenshot still works only once.

## Why switch from paper tickets and printed guest lists?

Paper tickets and printed lists are slow to check, easy to copy and hard to count. QR passes address all three problems while costing nothing to print.

| | Paper ticket or printed list | QR pass |
|---|---|---|
| Checking at the gate | Find the name or tear the stub by hand | Quick scan with a phone |
| Copies and duplicates | Photocopies are hard to spot | Second scan is flagged |
| Counting attendance | Manual tally after the event | Live check-in count |
| Lost ticket | Reprint or argue at the gate | Resend email or open pass page |
| Printing cost | Design and printing for every ticket | Optional; attendee can print their own |

## What do you need for QR check-in at the gate?

You need charged phones, a scanner your staff already know how to use, separate logins for each staff member, and a backup plan for slow networks and lost passes.

Before gates open, check that you have:

- **One phone per gate lane**, fully charged, with a power bank.
- **The scanner open and logged in** on every phone, tested with a sample pass.
- **Individual staff logins** rather than one shared account, so you know who scanned what.
- **A help desk** near the entrance for people who cannot find their pass, with access to the attendee list.
- **Good lighting at the gate.** QR codes scan poorly in the dark and under strong glare; ask attendees to turn up screen brightness.
- **A backup copy of the attendee list**, for example a CSV export saved on a laptop.
- **Power backup** (generator or inverter) if your gate area depends on venue electricity in the evening.

## What happens when mobile data is slow or drops?

Plan for it, because it happens. Mobile data often slows down when a large crowd gathers in one place, and some venues have weak coverage to begin with.

Practical ways to stay ready:

- **Use a scanner with an offline queue** if your event is large or the venue has poor signal. Scans are stored on the phone and synced when the connection returns.
- **Bring a dedicated hotspot or router for gate staff only**, separate from any public Wi-Fi.
- **Ask attendees to save their pass** as an image or screenshot before they leave home, so they do not need data at the gate.
- **Keep the exported attendee list** as a last resort for manual check-in.

One thing to know about any offline scanning: if two different gates are both offline and scan the same pass, the duplicate can only be detected after the scans sync. Keep your busiest gate online where possible.

## How do you set up multiple gates (entry, exit and VIP)?

Decide which ticket types may enter through which gate, then give each gate its own staff and short instructions. Even a small event benefits from a separate lane for guests, performers and VIPs.

Common setups:

- **Main entry** for General and Early Bird tickets.
- **Student gate** where staff check a student ID against Student tickets.
- **VIP or guest gate** for sponsors, chief guests and performers.
- **Exit gate** if you want to track who has left.

### Why track check-out as well as check-in?

Scanning people out as well gives you a live count of who is inside — useful for capacity in a banquet hall or auditorium, and for allowing re-entry.

## How do you handle duplicate or shared passes?

A good scanner flags a pass that has already been used. Your staff then need a calm, consistent way to deal with it.

- **Genuine re-entry:** if you allow re-entry, scan people out at the exit so their pass is valid again when they return.
- **Forwarded screenshot:** the second person is flagged. Staff can politely ask for the name on the pass and send them to the help desk.
- **Group bookings:** when one person buys several tickets, give each guest their own named pass so the group does not have to arrive together.

## How do you make event-day entry smooth?

Queues usually come from a few slow cases blocking a lane, so separate quick scans from problem cases.

- Send a reminder the day before with a link to each attendee's pass.
- Open gates early, and staff the busiest gate more heavily.
- Run a separate help-desk queue for lost passes, payment questions and name changes.
- If attendees pay at the venue, put the payment counter before or beside the gate, not inside the scanning lane. Our article on [selling event tickets online in Nepal](/blog/selling-event-tickets-online-nepal) covers pay-at-the-venue setups.
- If you accept walk-ins, keep registration open during the event and register them at the help desk.

## What should you do after the event?

Export the attendee list and check-in data, then compare registrations with actual check-ins. That tells you how many people registered but did not come, which ticket types filled up, and which gates were busiest.
