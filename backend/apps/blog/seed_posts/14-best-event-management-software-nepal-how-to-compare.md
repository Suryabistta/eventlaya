The best event management software in Nepal is the one that fits how your audience pays, how your gate runs and how much help your team needs — there is no single winner for every event. Compare tools on five things in this order: payments and payouts, fees, gate check-in, registration on a phone, and support and data ownership, then test your shortlist on a real phone before you decide.

This guide is a comparison framework, not a ranking. If you first want to know what event software does at all, start with our [complete guide to event management software in Nepal](/blog/event-management-software-nepal-guide) ([नेपालीमा पनि पढ्न सकिन्छ](/blog/nepal-ma-event-management-software)).

## What kinds of event tools do organizers in Nepal use?

Most organizers in Nepal use one of four kinds of tool, and many mix two of them. Knowing which kind you are looking at tells you what questions to ask.

| Type of tool | Typical use | Strengths | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Online forms + spreadsheet | Small free events, workshops, club meetings | Free, familiar, quick to set up | No tickets, QR passes, payment checks or gate scanner; manual work grows fast |
| Ticketing marketplace or wallet-run ticketing | Concerts, shows, movies, paid public events | Buyers may already use the app; integrated payment | Approval process, service charges, refund rules and payout terms are set by the platform |
| Global event platforms | International conferences, online events | Polished tools, many integrations | Check support for Nepali wallets, rupee pricing and payouts to a Nepali bank account |
| Event management software | Organizers who run their own registration and gate | Control over forms, ticket types, promo codes, gates and data | Plans and limits differ; payment options vary by tool |

Two real examples show why you must read each tool's own terms. Khalti's [event ticketing support page](https://khalti.com/info/support/event-ticketing/) says events are published after approval by Khalti, states a standard service charge of 20% of the total tickets sold through Khalti, and says sold tickets are non-refundable. [TicketChha](https://ticketchha.com/) lists Khalti, eSewa, ConnectIPS, Fonepay, mobile banking and cards at checkout and names Biratnagar and Dharan among its cities. Both statements are from their own pages at the time of writing; check the current versions before you rely on them.

## Which payment questions matter most?

Payments are where tools differ most in Nepal, so ask about money first. A feature list means little if the checkout does not match how your audience pays or if your money arrives weeks after the event.

Ask every tool these questions:

1. **Which methods can buyers use?** Khalti, eSewa, ConnectIPS, Fonepay, cards, or cash at the venue. Students and older audiences often pay differently.
2. **Whose merchant account collects the money?** Some platforms collect payments in their own account and pay you later; others connect to your own merchant account.
3. **When and how are you paid out?** Ask for the payout timing, method and any payout charge in writing.
4. **What does it cost per ticket?** Service charge or commission, gateway charges, and who pays them — you or the buyer.
5. **How are refunds handled?** Who returns the money, how long it takes, and whether fees are returned too.
6. **Can people still pay at the gate?** For walk-ins and cash payers, a pay-at-the-venue option avoids turning people away.
7. **What happens to unpaid bookings?** A good system holds the ticket for a short time while the buyer pays and releases it if payment fails, so a half-finished payment does not become a valid pass.

## What should you check at the gate?

Check how the tool behaves when hundreds of people arrive at once on weak mobile data. The gate is where a slow or confusing scanner turns into a long queue and angry guests.

- **Offline scanning:** does the scanner keep working and sync later if the data connection drops?
- **Duplicate alerts:** does a second scan of the same pass, or a screenshot shared with a friend, get flagged?
- **Multiple gates:** can you run separate entry, exit and VIP gates, and limit ticket types to certain gates?
- **Staff logins:** can volunteers scan with their own login, without access to your money or settings?
- **Check-out and live count:** can you see how many people are inside right now, for capacity and safety?
- **Unpaid passes:** does the scanner tell staff how much to collect, or refuse a pass whose online payment did not complete?

Print a few test passes and scan them on a cheap Android phone. Our guide to [QR code tickets and gate check-in](/blog/qr-code-tickets-gate-check-in-nepal) covers gate setup step by step.

## What should registration look like on a phone?

Registration should take a minute on a mid-range Android phone with mobile data, because that is how most attendees in Nepal will see it. Go through the whole flow yourself as a buyer.

Check that you can:

- add your own questions (college, T-shirt size, team name, town) and keep the form short;
- create ticket types with their own price, quantity and sales window (Early Bird, Student, VIP, free guest);
- use promo codes for discounts, partners or unlocking hidden tickets;
- let one buyer get several tickets in one order, with a name on each pass;
- write the event description, ticket names and questions in Nepali if your audience prefers it;
- show the pass on screen and send it by email, so it is not lost when someone deletes a message.

## How do you compare support, data and lock-in?

Treat support and data as part of the product. On event day you need answers within minutes, and after the event you need your attendee list.

- **Support:** who answers on event day, through which channel, and in which language? Is anyone nearby for a large event?
- **Data export:** can you download registrations, check-ins and payments as a CSV whenever you want?
- **Ownership:** read the privacy policy and terms to see who may use attendee data and for what.
- **Exit:** if you leave after one event, do you keep your lists and payment records?
- **Branding:** can you use your logo, or your own domain, if sponsors care about it?

## A scorecard you can fill in yourself

Use this scorecard to compare two or three tools honestly. Give each row a weight from 1 to 3 for your event, test each tool yourself, and score it 0 (no), 1 (partly) or 2 (yes). The numbers are yours; nobody else's ranking replaces your own test.

| Criterion | Question to ask | How to test it |
|---|---|---|
| Payment methods | Which wallets, banks, cards and cash options? | Buy a ticket yourself with each method you need |
| Payout | Who collects, when are you paid, at what charge? | Ask for the terms in writing |
| Fees | Plan price, per-ticket charge, gateway fee? | Calculate your total cost for one real event |
| Refunds | Who refunds, how fast, are fees returned? | Read the refund policy shown to buyers |
| Registration | Custom questions, ticket types, promo codes, group orders? | Register on a cheap phone with mobile data |
| Gate | Offline scanning, duplicates, several gates, staff logins? | Scan printed test passes, including one twice |
| Language | Can event text and questions be in Nepali? | Publish a test page in Nepali |
| Data | CSV export of attendees, check-ins and payments? | Export after your test registrations |
| Support | Who helps on event day, how, in which language? | Send a question and time the reply |
| Extras | Voting, branded site, team chat, venue booking? | Only score what your event really needs |

## Which extras are worth paying for?

Pay for extras only when your event uses them. Common ones in Nepal:

- **Audience voting and contests** for pageants, talent shows and college competitions, ideally with verified voters and judges' scoring.
- **A branded event site** on your own domain for festivals and recurring events.
- **Team tools** such as staff chat, task lists and announcements to gate staff.
- **A venue and vendor directory** to find banquets, sound, decoration and catering nearby.

## How do you run a fair trial before deciding?

Run the same small test on every tool on your shortlist. It takes an afternoon and saves you from finding problems on event day.

1. Create a test event with two ticket types and one promo code.
2. Register three times on different phones, including one group order.
3. Pay online once with a small amount, if the tool supports it, and check what the organizer sees.
4. Print the passes, scan them at two "gates" and scan one pass twice.
5. Export the attendee list and check that every field is there.
6. Ask support one real question and note how quickly and clearly they answer.

## Key takeaways

- There is no single best event management software in Nepal; the right tool depends on your payments, gate, team and budget.
- Ask about payments first: methods, whose account collects the money, payout timing, fees and refunds.
- Test the gate with printed passes, a duplicate scan and weak data, and test registration on a cheap phone.
