To sell concert tickets in Nepal, set clear ticket tiers with an early bird deadline, sell through one official link that takes online payment where possible, and give every buyer a unique QR pass that is scanned once at the gate. Sort out permissions, the venue capacity and your refund policy before tickets go on sale, and plan separate gates for VIP and general entry so the crowd moves quickly on the night.

## What do you need before concert tickets go on sale?

Have the basics confirmed in writing before you sell a single ticket. Selling first and confirming later is how concerts end up postponed with angry buyers.

Checklist:

- **Date and venue** booked, with the venue's safe capacity in writing.
- **Artist agreement** signed, including set length and arrival time.
- **Permissions** applied for (see below).
- **Ticket plan:** tiers, quantities, prices and sales dates.
- **Payment plan:** online, at the gate, or both.
- **Refund and postponement policy** written and ready to publish.
- **Gate plan:** how many gates, which tickets use which gate, and how many scanners.

## Which permissions does a concert need?

Most public concerts need the local authorities to know in advance, and large ones may need written approval. Requirements depend on the place, size and timing, so treat this as a starting list and confirm locally.

- **Ward office and municipality:** use of the venue or ground, especially public land, and any local fees.
- **Police:** security, crowd control and traffic around the venue. Large public gatherings may need approval at district level; ask the police which office applies to your event.
- **Sound and timing:** loudspeaker limits and the time the music must stop.
- **Safety:** fire safety, first aid or an ambulance on standby, and emergency exits that stay clear.
- **Banners and hoardings:** some municipalities require permission for street publicity.

Keep copies of every letter at the venue on the night.

## How should you structure concert ticket tiers?

Use a small number of clearly named tiers, each with its own quantity, so fans understand the choice in seconds. Too many tiers slow buyers down.

| Tier | Purpose | Gate | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early Bird | Reward early buyers, fund deposits | General | Sales window ends on a fixed date; announce it |
| General | Standard entry | General | Main tier, most of your capacity |
| Student | Lower price for students | General | Gate note: "Check student ID" |
| Front zone / fan pit | Closer to the stage | Front zone gate | Limited quantity; separate entrance to the zone |
| VIP | Seating, lounge or extras | VIP gate | Can be online-only to avoid cash at the VIP desk |
| Comps | Artists, sponsors, media | VIP or general | Hidden free tier, unlocked by code |

Set the quantity of each tier so the total never exceeds the venue's safe capacity. On Eventlaya, each ticket type has its own price, quantity, sales window and gate note, and you can limit a ticket type to certain gates so a General pass is refused at the VIP gate.

## How do you use promo codes for promoters, colleges and sponsors?

Give each partner their own code. You see who actually sells tickets, and you can limit or switch off any code without affecting the others.

- **Promoter codes:** a small percentage or rupee discount per promoter, with a usage limit and an expiry date.
- **College codes:** a code per college for student groups; useful for sponsor reports later.
- **Unlock codes:** codes that unlock a hidden ticket type, for comps, VIP invitations or sponsor allocations.

Keep a short list of who received which code. On Eventlaya, the attendee CSV export shows which promo code each ticket used, so you can settle up with promoters after the concert.

## Should fans pay online or at the gate?

For a paid concert, online payment is usually the safer default, with a gate counter for walk-ins if capacity allows. Each method has trade-offs.

| | Online (Khalti, eSewa) | Pay at the gate |
|---|---|---|
| Money in advance | Yes | No |
| No-show risk | Lower | Higher |
| Cash handling on the night | Little | A lot |
| Queue at the gate | Shorter | Longer, unless the counter is separate |
| Best for | Paid concerts, VIP and front-zone tiers | Low-priced shows, walk-ins |

## How do you stop fake and duplicate tickets?

Make every ticket unique and checkable at the gate, and sell from one official place. Most ticket fraud at concerts is simple copying or resale of the same pass.

- **Unique QR passes:** each pass has its own code and is accepted once; a second scan is flagged as a duplicate.
- **One official link:** publish the event link everywhere and say clearly that tickets bought elsewhere may not be valid.
- **Never post a real pass:** use a mock-up in promotions, not a screenshot of a valid pass.
- **Limit tickets per order:** a maximum per order makes bulk buying for resale harder.
- **Names on passes:** guest names on each pass help staff spot obvious resale.
- **Scan, don't glance:** staff must scan every pass, even ones that "look right" on screen.

## How do you plan the entry flow on concert night?

Plan the gate as a flow of people, not just a table with a scanner. Fans often arrive in a rush just before the headline act, so build for the peak, not the average.

1. **Open gates early** and announce the time, so arrivals spread out.
2. **Separate gates by tier:** VIP and front zone apart from general entry, each with its own scanner phones.
3. **Use at least two scanners per busy gate**, plus a spare phone and power bank.
4. **Put the pay-at-the-gate counter before the scanners**, so cash payment does not block scanning.
5. **Decide re-entry rules.** If fans may leave and return, scan them out at the exit; the live count of people inside then stays accurate.
6. **Watch the live count** against the venue's safe capacity and stop entry if you reach it.
7. **Brief staff on what to do** when a pass is a duplicate, unpaid or for the wrong gate.

## What should your refund and postponement policy say?

Publish the policy on the event page before sales open. Weather, strikes, artist travel and official decisions can all move a concert date in Nepal.

Include:

- whether tickets are refundable, and until when;
- what happens if the concert is postponed: do tickets move to the new date automatically?
- what happens if it is cancelled: how and when buyers are refunded;
- whether tickets can be transferred to another person;
- who to contact, and how.

If you take online payments through a platform, check how that platform handles refunds before you write your policy.

## How do you promote ticket sales?

Give fans one link and one reason to buy now. Share the official event link in every post, story and message, and use the early bird deadline as your main push. Ask the artists to share the link, and give partner colleges their own codes. If low numbers would put people off, you can hide how many spots are left; if scarcity helps, show it.

## Key takeaways

- Confirm venue capacity, permissions, artist agreement and refund policy before selling.
- Use a few clear tiers with fixed quantities, an early bird deadline and gate rules per tier.
- Give each promoter, college and sponsor their own code, and use hidden tiers for comps.
- Take online payment where you can, with a separate gate counter for walk-ins.
- Unique QR passes, one official link and scanning every pass stop most fake tickets.
