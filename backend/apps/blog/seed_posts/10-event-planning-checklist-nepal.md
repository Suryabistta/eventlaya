A good event planning checklist for Nepal works backwards from your event date: fix the date and budget first, book the venue and key vendors next, then handle registration, promotion, staffing and event-day logistics. In Nepal, the calendar matters as much as the checklist — Dashain and Tihar, the Mangsir and Magh–Falgun wedding seasons, and the monsoon all affect venue availability, vendor schedules and how many people can attend.

## When should you start planning an event?

For a small workshop or meetup, three to four weeks can be enough. For a college fest, concert, conference or large community event, start two to three months ahead, and earlier if your date is near a festival or in the wedding season.

The bigger the crowd and the more vendors involved, the more lead time you need. Venues and sound or stage vendors are usually the first things to get booked out.

## What does an event planning timeline look like?

Use this timeline as a starting point and shorten it for smaller events.

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| 3 months before | Set goals, audience size and budget. Pick a date after checking the festival and exam calendar. Form your core team and assign roles. |
| 2–3 months before | Shortlist and book the venue. Book key vendors: sound and stage, lighting, photography, catering, decoration. Approach sponsors. |
| 6–8 weeks before | Create the event page, ticket types and registration form. Start promotion. Inform the ward office or municipality and local police if it is a large public event. |
| 4 weeks before | Confirm performers, speakers and judges. Plan gate layout, ticket checks and crowd flow. Arrange power backup. |
| 2 weeks before | Send reminders to registrants. Finalize the run sheet and stage schedule. Confirm all vendors in writing, with arrival times. |
| 1 week before | Brief volunteers and gate staff. Test the check-in scanner with sample passes. Prepare signage and a help desk. |
| Day before | Site visit and setup. Sound check. Charge every phone and power bank. Send a final reminder with the pass link and directions. |
| Event day | Open gates early. Keep a help desk for lost passes and payments. Track check-ins and the headcount inside. |
| After the event | Pay vendors, settle the budget, thank sponsors and attendees, export the attendee data, and note lessons for next time. |

## What should be on the pre-event checklist?

Before you promote anything, make sure the basics are settled. Changing a date or venue after tickets are sold creates confusion and refunds.

**Planning and budget**

- Event goal and target audience.
- Expected number of attendees.
- Budget with a contingency line for surprises.
- A named person responsible for each area: venue, vendors, registration, promotion, gate, stage.

**Venue and vendors**

- Venue capacity, parking, toilets, power backup, accessibility and sound restrictions.
- Written confirmation of booking, dates and what is included.
- Vendor contacts and arrival times on one shared sheet.

**Permissions and safety**

- Inform the ward office or municipality and local police for large public events; rules vary, so check locally.
- First-aid arrangements and a plan for emergencies.
- Crowd capacity and entry/exit routes.

## How do Dashain and Tihar affect event planning?

Dashain and Tihar usually fall between late September and November, and they change everything about availability. Many people travel to their home districts, offices and colleges close, and vendors are busy with festive bookings.

- **Avoid festival weeks for conferences, college fests and competitions.** Attendance drops when students and staff are away.
- **Plan festive events early.** If you are organizing a Dashain or Tihar programme, cultural night or Deusi-Bhailo event, book venues and sound well in advance.
- **Expect travel disruptions.** Buses and roads are crowded before and after the festivals; performers and vendors coming from other towns may be delayed.
- **Confirm vendor availability in writing**, including whether staff will be working during the festival.
- **Mind Chhath in the Terai**, which follows Tihar and is a major festival in towns across the plains.

## How does the wedding season affect venues and vendors?

The main wedding seasons — around Mangsir (November–December) and Magh–Falgun (January–March) — are when banquets, party palaces, caterers, decorators and photographers book up early. If your event falls in these months, book sooner than you think you need to.

- Ask venues about auspicious wedding dates, which fill first.
- Consider weekday events if weekends are fully booked.
- Lock in prices and inclusions in writing.
- Have a backup venue in mind.

## What should you consider about weather?

Weather planning in Nepal is mostly about the monsoon and the heat. The monsoon runs roughly from June to September and brings heavy rain, flooding and road disruptions.

- **Monsoon:** avoid fully open-air events, or have a covered backup. Check access roads, especially for participants travelling from the hills.
- **Pre-monsoon (April–May):** the Terai can be very hot; plan shade, water and fans, and consider evening timings.
- **Winter in the hills:** evenings in places like Dhankuta or Ilam get cold; tell attendees to bring warm clothes for outdoor programmes.

## What should you check in the final week?

The final week is about confirmation and rehearsal. Nothing new should be decided now.

- Confirm every vendor, performer and speaker again.
- Brief volunteers and gate staff on their roles and logins.
- Test the scanner, the sound system and the projector.
- Print signage, a run sheet and an emergency contact list.
- Send attendees directions, parking information and a pass reminder.
- Plan for slow mobile data with offline backups. Our guide to [QR code tickets and gate check-in](/blog/qr-code-tickets-gate-check-in-nepal) covers gate setup in detail.

## What does an event-day checklist include?

On the day, your job is coordination, not doing every task yourself. Keep one person free to solve problems.

- Gate staff in position before gates open, with charged phones.
- Help desk for lost passes, payments and questions.
- Power backup tested and fuelled.
- Stage schedule visible to the host and backstage team.
- A designated person tracking headcount and safety.
- Someone collecting photos and short videos for later promotion.

## Key takeaways

- Start two to three months ahead for medium and large events, and earlier during festival and wedding seasons.
- Check the calendar for Dashain, Tihar, Chhath, wedding seasons and exams before fixing a date.
- Book venues and sound and stage vendors first; confirm everything in writing.
- Plan for monsoon rain, power cuts and slow mobile data.
- Debrief after the event and save your checklist for next time.
