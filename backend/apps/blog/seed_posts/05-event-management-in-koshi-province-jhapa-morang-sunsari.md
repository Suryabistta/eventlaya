Organizing events across Koshi Province means planning for two very different landscapes: busy Terai towns in Jhapa, Morang and Sunsari along the East-West Highway, and hill towns such as Ilam and Dhankuta where travel takes longer and the weather changes faster. In smaller towns, the basics stay the same (venue, permission, registration, check-in), but you rely more on local partners, book vendors from bigger cities earlier, and plan for audiences that travel in from surrounding villages.

## How is organizing an event in a smaller Koshi town different?

The main differences are fewer local vendors, more travel for your audience, and a stronger role for local partners. In cities like Biratnagar, Itahari and Dharan you can find most services within a few kilometres. In smaller towns, you may need to bring sound, stage, decoration or photography from elsewhere.

What that means in practice:

- **Book vendors early**, especially if they travel from a bigger city, and confirm transport and setup times in writing.
- **Partner with local groups** such as clubs, schools, colleges, youth organizations and business associations. They know the venues and the audience.
- **Talk to the ward office and palika early.** In smaller municipalities, local offices are often closely involved in public events.
- **Expect a wider catchment.** People may come from nearby villages and towns, so publish start and end times clearly.

For bigger-city guides, see [how to organize an event in Itahari](/blog/how-to-organize-an-event-in-itahari), [Dharan](/blog/how-to-organize-an-event-in-dharan) and [Biratnagar](/blog/how-to-organize-an-event-in-biratnagar).

## How do you plan events in the Jhapa towns?

Plan Jhapa events around the highway towns, which act as gathering points for the district. Damak and Birtamod are natural choices for district-wide programmes, Kakarbhitta for events connected to trade and the border, and Bhadrapur when guests need an airport.

Tips:

- Use a registration question like "Which town are you coming from?" to understand your catchment.
- In Kakarbhitta, allow for traffic around the border crossing at peak times.
- For guests flying into Bhadrapur, arrange pickups and keep a flexible agenda slot in case flights change.
- Check for Chhath and other Terai festival dates before fixing your day.

## How do you plan events in Morang's smaller towns?

Work through local institutions. Urlabari, Pathari, Belbari and Rangeli are smaller than Biratnagar, so schools, colleges, clubs and community organizations are often the best partners for venues, volunteers and promotion.

- Community centers, school halls and open grounds are common choices for programmes in smaller towns.
- For outdoor events, avoid the monsoon months and the hottest pre-monsoon afternoons.
- Ask partners to share your event link in their WhatsApp and Viber groups; word of mouth carries further in smaller communities.
- If sound and stage come from Biratnagar or Itahari, confirm arrival times and the power supply at the venue.

## How do you plan events in the hills, such as Ilam and Dhankuta?

Give hill events more lead time and a stronger weather plan. Hill roads take longer, monsoon rain can disrupt travel, and evenings get cold in winter.

| Planning area | Hill-town consideration |
|---|---|
| Travel | Allow extra time for vendors and guests on hill roads |
| Season | Avoid the monsoon (roughly June–September) for outdoor events |
| Weather | Winter evenings are cool; tell attendees to dress warmly |
| Equipment | Bring spares (cables, batteries, extension cords); shops may be limited |
| Power | Confirm a generator or inverter at the venue |
| Connectivity | Mobile data can be patchy; plan for offline check-in |

Ilam's tea landscapes and Dhankuta's hill setting can make outdoor events memorable in the dry season. Just keep an indoor backup.

## What about Inaruwa and Gaighat?

Inaruwa, as Sunsari's district headquarters, suits official, district-level and institutional programmes. Gaighat, in Triyuga Municipality in Udayapur, is further from the main Sunsari–Morang cluster, so plan transport carefully if your audience or vendors are coming from Itahari or Biratnagar.

For both, confirm venue capacity and whether vendors will travel in or are available locally.

## How do you manage registration and check-in across several towns?

Use a separate event page for each town but the same registration form and ticket types. That keeps each venue's numbers clean and lets you compare towns afterwards.

1. Create one event per town and venue, with its own date and capacity.
2. Reuse the same form questions so your data lines up.
3. Give each town's local partner their own promo code to track who brought registrations.
4. Assign gate staff from the local partner and brief them on the scanner before the day.
5. Export each event's attendee list as CSV and compare.

## Key takeaways

- Koshi's Terai highway towns and hill towns need different plans for travel, weather and vendors.
- In smaller towns, local partners, the ward office and the palika matter more than in big cities.
- Book vendors from bigger cities early and confirm transport, setup and power in writing.
- For hill events, add lead time, avoid the monsoon and prepare for cool evenings and weak data.
- For multi-town tours, use one event per town with a shared form and per-partner promo codes.
